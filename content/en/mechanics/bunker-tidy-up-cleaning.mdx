## Why Route Planning Beats Raw Speed

Most players start by grabbing whatever is closest. That instinct creates the worst possible pattern: the pile in front of you empties out, but everything behind it stays blocked, and you never build a mental map of where each category lives. Community reports from large sorting scenarios consistently point to the same fix — open lanes first, organize second.

A single clear walking path between the middle of the room and the shelf wall is worth more than a finished category early on. That path lets you see the actual shape of the mess, spot category boundaries, and reach distant storage without climbing over boxes. Visibility, not speed, is what makes the later phases fast.

| Cleanup element | Practical role | Recommended approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floor clutter | Main source of visible mess | Clear broad lanes before sorting anything |
| Storage shelves | Final destination for every item | Learn category placement early |
| Cleanup skills | Move matching groups faster | Save them for dense, obvious clusters |
| Hidden collectibles | Optional search objective | Sweep for them after the floor opens up |
| Final items | Small, easy-to-miss objects | Switch to manual inspection |

The opening principle is simple: never chase an isolated item while large piles still block your view of the room. Clear a big section, then use that open floor to plan.

## The Five-Phase Cleanup Order That Works

The bunker's storage is divided into recognizable groups — books, survival supplies, weapons and protection, pet food, household chemicals, general supplies, and food. Those groupings should guide your route even when the floor looks completely chaotic at the start.

Large objects and dense piles are the right early targets because removing them improves sightlines. Once the central floor is clearer, smaller items such as batteries, matches, toiletries, canned goods, medicine, and individual tools finally become identifiable.

| Phase | Main objective | Typical targets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1 | Open movement lanes | Bulky floor piles, obvious containers |
| Phase 2 | Establish categories | Books, food, survival goods, hygiene items |
| Phase 3 | Consolidate groups | Canned food, pet food, medicine, batteries |
| Phase 4 | Inspect edges | Chairs, corners, tables, shelf gaps, carpet borders |
| Phase 5 | Final sweep | Single items, hidden collectibles, buried objects |

**1. Choose one room edge.** Start along an outer wall or a clear corner. Working from an edge stops you from repeatedly crossing the same cluttered center and gives you a fixed reference point for the rest of the run.

**2. Create a central route.** Remove just enough to form a walking path between the main floor and the shelves. A usable route improves access to every later group, which is why it beats finishing one category immediately.

**3. Group by destination.** Pick up items that belong to the same shelf area. Food, medicine, books, pet supplies, hygiene products, and survival goods should be handled as separate rounds, not mixed loads.

**4. Use skills on dense clusters.** Many items look nearly identical at a glance, so confirm the destination before committing to a large automated pickup. Hauling a mixed load across the room just creates extra trips.

**5. Slow down at the end.** Once only a few hundred objects remain, the density advantage disappears and manual sorting becomes more reliable.

## Skills, Movement, and Inventory Management

Cleanup skills are the main efficiency tools in this scenario. Some abilities collect matching items, while others improve carrying capacity, movement speed, shelf interaction, or item visibility. Their value shifts constantly during a run: an ability that is essential while the bunker is packed may be dead weight when only a handful of objects remain.

A high-capacity pickup is strongest when many identical objects sit in one spot. It is far weaker for unique items, hidden objects, or supplies spread across separate rooms. The most efficient approach alternates between skill use and manual sorting instead of committing to either method for the entire cleanup.

| Skill or tool type | Best use | Weak use case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matching pickup | Dense groups of identical items | Mixed piles with lookalike objects |
| Increased carry capacity | Long routes to distant shelves | Short trips with nearby destinations |
| Faster movement | Crossing between storage zones | Sorting items already beside the shelf |
| Shelf matching | Learning unfamiliar destinations | Late cleanup when categories are known |
| Manual pickup | Final objects and isolated items | Clearing the whole room from the start |

**Large-group strategy**

- Collect visible stacks of the same item.
- Activate a matching skill only after confirming the category.
- Deposit the load before switching targets.

**Small-group strategy**

- Pick up isolated objects by hand.
- Check nearby corners and furniture while you are there.
- Avoid spending a long cooldown on a single item.

**Final-sweep strategy**

- Turn off assistance skills you no longer need.
- Inspect floor edges, carpet borders, and shelf gaps.
- Use the shelf list to identify which item type is still missing.

## Category Guide and Hidden-Item Search

The supply list is deliberately varied, and a few categories are easy to confuse. Drinks may be stored separately from survival supplies, and household products can sit surprisingly close to food or medicine. Use shelf labels and item silhouettes rather than relying on color alone — many packages share the same palette.

| Category | Examples | Search clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food | Canned meat, fruit, vegetables, soup, jam | Check lower shelves and dense food piles |
| Drinks | Soda, juice, energy drinks, beer | Separate beverage areas are easy to overlook |
| Survival | Energy bars, matches, batteries, radios | Often mixed in with small utility items |
| Hygiene | Shampoo, toothpaste, soap, razors, toothbrushes | Small objects frequently remain near edges |
| Medicine | Painkillers, bandages, iodine, first-aid kits | Inspect corners and partly cleared piles |
| Pet supplies | Cat food and dog food | Look for repeated stacks on upper shelves |
| Reading material | Books and magazines | Usually grouped in one distinct shelf section |

Hidden rubber ducks are the other major objective. They can be buried inside piles, tucked beside furniture, or placed where their bright color blends into surrounding clutter. Community reports suggest searching for them *after* the main floor has opened up, then revisiting dense zones that still contain unexplained objects.

That order matters. Searching for collectibles at the very beginning means digging through piles twice — once to find the duck, and again to actually clear the supplies around it.

## Completion Checklist and Final Sweep

A finished cleanup is more than a tidy-looking center. Small objects can stay hidden under visual clutter, beside furniture, or along the edge of a carpet. The final check should combine the item counter, shelf status, category matching, and one slow visual pass around the room.

| Completion check | What to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| Item counter | No uncollected items remain |
| Shelf status | Every storage section accepts its categories |
| Floor inspection | No isolated item hidden near furniture |
| Collectibles | All hidden rubber ducks located |
| Room boundaries | Doors and exits have been checked |
| Skill status | Remaining upgrades used or intentionally saved |

**The final sweep, in order:**

- Clear the main walking routes and room edges.
- Sort every visible food and survival group.
- Check medicine, hygiene, and pet-supply shelves.
- Search corners, furniture, and carpet borders for stragglers.
- Locate every hidden duck before leaving.

Use a category filter or matching indicator to confirm what remains, then inspect the area where that item type was last seen. This is dramatically faster than scanning the entire bunker at random. Once the storage is organized, also check the room boundaries and exit area for any post-cleanup interaction — but inspect the actual interaction prompts rather than assuming every visible door leads somewhere new.

## FAQ

**What is the main goal of bunker tidy up cleaning?**

The goal is to sort scattered supplies into their correct storage areas. There is no combat or character progression here — the real challenge is organization, route planning, and finding small items buried under large piles.

**How many items are in the main bunker scenario?**

The featured bunker contains 17,437 items according to the game's promotional information. Your actual completion time depends heavily on how often you use skills and how thoroughly you search the edges of the room.

**Should I activate cleanup skills immediately?**

Use them when a large group of matching items is visible and its destination is clear. Keep some flexibility for dense late-game clusters, but switch to manual pickup once only isolated objects remain, since cooldowns are wasted on single items.

**Where should I look for hidden rubber ducks?**

Search dense piles, corners, furniture edges, and spots that were hard to inspect before the floor was cleared. A slow final sweep during bunker tidy up cleaning is far more reliable than searching randomly at the beginning.

A clean route, clear category groups, and a deliberate final pass will always beat rushing. Treat the bunker as a series of zones rather than one giant pile, and the item counter will drop faster than you expect.
