## What to Track at Every Shelf Location

The most common failure in Bunker Tidy Up is not missing a shelf — it is forgetting which shelves you already handled. Memory does not survive a walk between rooms, and two similar corridors can blur into one.

Record three things for every storage area: where it is, what category of item it holds, and whether it has been fully checked. A shelf that looks empty may still belong to a larger storage section, while a partially sorted shelf can make a finished room look unfinished.

| Field | What to record | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Area name | Room, corridor, corner, or storage zone | Prevents duplicate searches |
| Shelf identifier | Left, center, right, upper, or lower | Separates shelves that sit inches apart |
| Item group | Batteries or another visible category | Keeps sorting consistent across rooms |
| Status | Unchecked, active, sorted, verified, pending | Shows the next task at a glance |
| Notes | Missing item, blocked view, or revisit reason | Preserves clues for later sessions |

Use a fixed naming pattern such as **Room A - Shelf 01** or **Storage West - Upper Rack**. The exact words matter less than consistency. When several zones share the same layout, a stable label is the only reliable way to tell them apart.

### Room Sweep, Shelf Pass, Verification Pass

- **Room sweep:** enter, follow one wall, and finish the visible shelf line before changing direction.
- **Shelf pass:** inspect each shelf from top to bottom, then log it right away.
- **Verification pass:** revisit only the flagged shelves instead of restarting the whole bunker.

Community reports consistently show that the verification pass is the step players skip — and the one that causes most duplicated counting.

## Route Planning: How to Cover Every Location Without Doubling Back

A route fixes the most common shelf-location mistake: checking the same corner three times while an entire section goes untouched. The sequence below does not depend on a specific room layout, so it keeps working as your map knowledge grows.

1. **Pick a landmark.** Use the main entrance, a wide doorway, or a distinctive piece of equipment. That landmark is where your route resets.
2. **Clear one side first.** Follow the left or right wall and inspect every visible shelf before crossing the room. Crossing back and forth creates gaps.
3. **Keep one inspection direction.** Read each shelf top-to-bottom or bottom-to-top, but never switch halfway. Log the shelf immediately after inspecting it.
4. **Flag anything uncertain.** If an object or angle blocks a confident check, mark the shelf pending. Pending is always safer than calling it empty.
5. **Close the loop.** Return to your starting landmark and compare the room against your log before moving on.

| Route phase | What you do | Completion signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Choose a landmark and label the zone | Starting point recorded |
| First pass | Follow one wall or aisle | Every visible shelf has a status |
| Detail check | Inspect upper, middle, and lower segments | No shelf segment skipped |
| Exception pass | Revisit blocked or uncertain shelves | Pending entries resolved |
| Exit check | Compare the room against the log | Route returns to its landmark |

When a room holds several shelves close together, split it into smaller zones. Labels like **north wall**, **center rack**, and **door-side shelf** isolate a missing item far faster than one broad label such as "storage room."

### Avoid False Completion

Do not mark a shelf done just because the front row looks neat. Hidden, elevated, or obstructed sections stay pending until you can inspect them confidently. Community reports on Bunker Tidy Up repeatedly mention this: a tidy front row can hide an entire unfinished layer behind it.

## Sorting Priorities for Batteries and Shelf Groups

The best sorting system is the one you can repeat without thinking. Start with items that are visible and easy to place, then use the leftover shelf space to expose what is still missing. Moving items randomly between rooms makes your log unreadable.

For the battery objective, a three-stage priority works well:

1. Collect every visible battery in the active zone.
2. Place it in the correct shelf group or a clearly labeled working pile.
3. Verify the shelf after placement so the same area is not counted twice.

| Priority | Task | Recommended action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | Visible battery in the active zone | Collect and log immediately |
| High | Shelf with uncertain status | Mark pending and inspect before leaving |
| Medium | Partially organized shelf | Finish the section before expanding |
| Medium | Nearby loose items | Group by visible type or shelf purpose |

Verified means you came back and confirmed nothing visible was missed. Promote a shelf to verified only after you finish the room's full route.
