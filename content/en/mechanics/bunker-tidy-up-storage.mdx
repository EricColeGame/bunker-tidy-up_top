## Why Storage Planning Beats Speed in Bunker Tidy Up

Bunker Tidy Up is billed as a relaxing organization game, which is precisely why it tempts players to wing it. The core loop — pick up an item, identify its category, place it in suitable storage — sounds trivial until dozens of shelves are competing for the same supplies. Grabbing the nearest box and hunting for a gap feels productive for the first ten minutes. By the third hour, it recreates the exact mess you set out to fix.

A storage plan removes repeated decision-making. Instead of asking "where does this go?" for all 17,437 items, you answer that question once per category and then move on. Player experience shared around the community consistently points to the same pattern: the people who lock in shelf rules early finish cleanup runs far faster than those who keep rearranging later.

The wiki's beginner guidance breaks the loop into three habits worth copying:

- **Survey the bunker before moving anything.** Group nearby items mentally so every trip has a purpose.
- **Pick up and identify one item at a time.** Compare against nearby objects and judge by function, not appearance, when an item is ambiguous.
- **Place consistently and finish the area.** Clear a whole section before starting the next one.

### The Four Core Categories

Nearly everything in the bunker falls into one of four recognizable groups. Learned early, these become the backbone of any bunker tidy up storage layout.

| Category | Typical items | Where it belongs | Sorting tip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Food | Rations, drinks, consumables, food storage | Provision shelves or pantry zone | Sort by container type first, then by size |
| Medicine | First-aid, treatment, recovery supplies | Dedicated medical shelf or cabinet | Keep the entire medical group in one row |
| Tools | Repair, maintenance, build, equipment-handling gear | Tool shelf or workshop area | Classify by what the item does, not how it looks |
| Books | Reading and reference material | Bookcase or library section | Use purpose, not size, to decide membership |

Hidden objects complicate this. The wiki notes that many supplies are tucked behind shelves, boxes, and corners, so checking storage areas carefully while you clean matters as much as the placement itself. Skipping those checks means re-sorting the same shelf twice.

## Bunker Tidy Up Storage Zones: Map the Bunker Before You Move Anything

Zoning is the difference between organized storage and a shelf that merely looks neat. Give each section a single purpose, leave visible gaps between categories, and keep frequently handled items at accessible heights. Community reports from players deep into their cleanup runs suggest the same trick repeatedly: build a rough map of the bunker on paper or in your head before touching a single crate.

| Zone | Suggested location | Holds | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry staging | Just inside the main door or corridor | Unsorted items during a first pass | Keeps clutter off the main walkways |
| Provision wall | Shelves closest to the living area | Food, drinks, consumables | Puts the most-used category within easy reach |
| Medical row | One dedicated cabinet or shelf run | Medicine and first-aid supplies | Fast visual checks, nothing mixed in |
| Tool corner | Near the workshop or maintenance area | Tools and repair equipment | Keeps heavy, bulky items close to where they are used |
| Library section | A bookcase at the quieter end | Books and reading materials | Strong visual consistency and easy gap checks |
| Deep storage | Less central rooms and back walls | Oversized items, spares, low-priority supplies | Moves rarely touched goods out of the way |

Two zoning habits pay off disproportionately. First, reserve extra space in each zone rather than filling every slot — supplies keep arriving, and an overstuffed shelf forces you to relocate an entire category later. Second, use visual landmarks: a consistent shelf height, a color of crate, or a visible gap between groups means you can identify a category at a glance instead of inspecting every label.

## Skills That Make Bunker Tidy Up Storage Faster

Skill upgrades shape how quickly a storage plan converts into a finished bunker. The wiki outlines three that matter most, and each one solves a different bottleneck. Choosing the wrong one early is the most common reason players feel stuck.

| Skill | What it does | Best for | Priority window |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity | Carry more items per trip | Players who constantly walk back to storage after a few pickups | High early, while loose items are spread everywhere |
| Carry Speed | Move items faster while transporting | Players with enough space who still spend most of their time walking | High when storage zones sit far apart |
| Cleaning Efficiency | Speeds up later cleanup work | Players pushing through large remaining sections | Best after carrying and movement are handled |

A practical order for most layouts: take Capacity first, because early clutter is spread across the bunker and trip count is the real enemy. Shift into Carry Speed once your zones are established but located in separate rooms. Save Cleaning Efficiency for the phase when the main sorting is done and only the long tail remains.

Some players also mention magnet-style tools that help gather nearby items in a single pull. Treat that as community reporting rather than a guaranteed mechanic — check your in-game upgrade list before planning around it.

## A Repeatable Bunker Tidy Up Storage Routine

Progress is easiest when each stage has one clear purpose. The route below compresses the wiki's progression guidance into six phases that work in any bunker layout.

| Phase | Goal | Rule of thumb |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Clear main routes | Open entrances, corridors, and work areas | Everything movable leaves the walkways first |
| 2. Establish broad groups | Assign equipment, supplies, and miscellaneous homes | Broad categories before fine detail |
| 3. First sorting pass | Move every visible item into the right group | Coverage over perfect spacing |
| 4. Refine placement | Improve spacing and accessibility | Frequently used items stay reachable |
| 5. Final walkthrough | Check every room and storage section | No misplaced items, no blocked doors |
| 6. Maintain the layout | Return new items immediately | A short pass prevents a full re-clean |

Batching is what makes this routine fast. When a large supply drop arrives, do not place items one by one into final positions. Instead:

- Create temporary piles for equipment, consumables, tools, and miscellaneous goods.
- Place urgent or frequently used items first, while your energy and attention are highest.
- Run the alignment and spacing pass only after everything is broadly categorized.

Keep in mind that placement controls matter here. Positioning an item carefully before confirming a drop avoids blocking storage access, and clear walkways make every later sorting pass easier — a point the game's control guidance emphasizes for both keyboard and controller players.

## Common Bunker Tidy Up Storage Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Even careful players fall into the same traps. Each one is cheap to fix early and expensive to fix late.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | The fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creating too many micro-categories | Every new group adds a decision and a location to remember | Merge isolated items into an existing category |
| Filling every shelf immediately | New supplies force a full category relocation | Leave breathing room in each zone |
| Rearranging mid-pass | You re-sort the same items repeatedly | Finish the first pass before refining anything |
| Parking piles in doorways | Blocked paths slow every future trip | Use a staging zone away from main routes |
| Ignoring hidden supplies | Items behind shelves resurface after you "finish" | Check behind boxes and corners during cleanup |
| Skipping the final walkthrough | Mixed categories quietly reappear | Do one entrance-to-back-wall inspection |

The maintenance phase is where most bunkers fall apart. Once your layout is finished, new items should go straight to their assigned zone rather than into a temporary pile. If several supplies arrive together, run the batch method again instead of improvising. ## FAQ: Bunker Tidy Up Storage Questions

**How should I start organizing storage in Bunker Tidy Up?**
Survey the room first, then pick up items one at a time and identify which of the four main categories each belongs to — food, medicine, tools, or books. Place them in the most suitable storage area and finish that section before moving on. Consistent placement from the start prevents repeated sorting later.

**How many supplies does Bunker Tidy Up have to store?**
The community wiki lists 17,437 supplies to discover, sort, and arrange across the bunker's storage areas. Because that number is so large, category rules and zones matter more than individual placement decisions.

**What is the best first upgrade for bunker tidy up storage work?**
Capacity is usually the strongest early pick, since loose items are scattered across the bunker and trip count is your biggest time cost. Move into Carry Speed once your zones are set but spread far apart, and take Cleaning Efficiency later for the final cleanup push.

**Do hidden objects change how I should plan storage?**
Yes. Many supplies sit behind shelves, boxes, and corners, so plan a little spare room in each zone. If a shelf is packed tight, you will have nowhere to put the items you uncover during the final walkthrough.
