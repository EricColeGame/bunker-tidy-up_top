## The Category Map: Downstairs vs. Upstairs

Think of the bunker as two destinations instead of one enormous mess. Downstairs shelves handle anything edible or drinkable. Upstairs shelves handle anything related to survival, cleanliness, or pet care. Once you internalize that split, a large share of your trips become obvious before you even pick an item up.

### Downstairs: Food, Drinks, and Pantry Items

*   Canned meals, canned fruit, vegetables, and meat
*   Juice, soda, beer, and energy drinks
*   Peanut butter, jam, sugar, and spices
*   Mayonnaise and similar sauces
*   Crackers and dry snack goods

## Confusing Items and Category Exceptions

A handful of items break the "it looks like this, so it must go there" instinct. Mayonnaise is the classic example: most players file it mentally with condiments, but the game treats it as a sauce and expects it on the sauce shelf. Energy drinks and energy bars split in the opposite direction — the drink goes downstairs with beverages, while the bar counts as a survival item and belongs upstairs.

These exceptions matter because a rejected stack means a wasted trip across the entire bunker. Confirm the shelf category before you drop a pile, and if an item will not settle, recheck the label instead of forcing it into a similar-looking spot.

| Common mistake | What happens | How to fix it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Filing mayonnaise as a condiment | The stack will not register on the wrong shelf | Treat it as a sauce, downstairs |
| Sorting energy bars with drinks | Bars stay loose and clutter the drink zone | Move them to the upstairs survival section |
| Collecting batteries one at a time | Hundreds of trips across two floors | Batch them with search or magnet abilities |
| Skipping pet food | Leftovers linger on the upper floor | Route all cat and dog food upstairs early |
| Trusting appearance alone | Repeated wrong-shelf errors | Verify the category family first |

Quantities in community playthroughs are worth knowing as checkpoints. ## A Step-by-Step Bunker Tidy Up Item Placement Route

Random scanning is the biggest time sink in the entire game. A fixed route keeps the floor readable and prevents you from walking past the same object three times. This sequence works because it clears visibility first, then reduces decision-making, then handles the messy remainder.

1.  **Clear the main walking paths.** Pick up anything around tables, chairs, major shelves, and the central floor. Open ground makes later searches faster and reduces the chance of missing an item tucked behind a large stack.
2.  **Split the loose objects into two broad groups.** Downstairs items — food, drinks, sauces, spices — go first. Then handle upstairs items in dedicated trips: survival goods, hygiene products, household supplies, and pet food.
3.  **Target one item type at a time.** Pick batteries, matches, dog food, or canned fruit, use the search function to reveal every match, collect a reasonable stack, and deliver it in one run.
4.  **Use collection abilities on dense piles.** Magnet-style abilities earn their keep on tiny or numerous objects such as batteries, matches, painkillers, and bandages that are hard to distinguish visually.
5.  **Finish with manual inspection.** When only a few hundred items remain, stop relying on abilities and walk each section slowly. This is how you uncover objects hidden beneath furniture or pressed into crowded piles.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening pass | Clear open floor areas | Improves visibility and movement |
| Category pass | Move food, drinks, and supplies separately | Reduces destination confusion |
| Search pass | Target a single item type | Prevents random scanning |
| Dense-pile pass | Use collection abilities | Saves time on small objects |
| Final pass | Inspect manually | Finds isolated or hidden items |

## Search Abilities, Dense Piles, and Hidden Objects

Late-game cleanup is less about muscle and more about patience. Community reports describe a final missing painkiller turning up underneath a carpet rather than on any shelf, which is why a floor that looks finished can still hold one stubborn object. Reserve search and magnet abilities for the moments when you know the item type but cannot spot it inside a thick pile.

| Problem | Best response | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Too many tiny objects | Search and collect in batches | Picking up every battery individually |
| Item seems missing | Check under furniture and carpet edges | Assuming the shelf is wrong |
| Wrong destination | Recheck the category family | Trusting appearance alone |
| One object remains | Scan floors slowly and methodically | Declaring the room empty |

A few habits make the search system more dependable:

*   Search before walking every pile.
*   Keep the selected item visible while scanning.
*   Check the floor directly beneath your character.
*   Inspect carpet edges, corners, and furniture undersides.
*   Revisit dense piles after the surrounding area is clear.

## Bunker Tidy Up Item Placement FAQ

**What is the fastest way to learn bunker tidy up item placement?**

Learn the two-floor split first: food and drinks downstairs, survival goods, hygiene products, and pet food upstairs. From there, memorize category families rather than exact shelf positions, and confirm a shelf's label before dropping a stack. This approach prevents most repeated trips across the bunker.

**Where do energy drinks and energy bars go?**

Energy drinks are sorted with beverages downstairs, while energy bars count as survival supplies and belong upstairs. Separating these two item types early prevents one of the most common sorting mistakes in the game.

**What is the most efficient way to handle 420 batteries?**

Do not collect them individually. Use the item search function to reveal every battery, gather a manageable stack, and return them together. Magnet-style abilities help when the pile becomes too dense to read visually.

**What should I do when one item is still missing?**

Inspect low-visibility surfaces first: carpet edges, corners, furniture undersides, and crowded piles. Player experience repeatedly shows that the final object is often partially hidden rather than sitting in plain sight on a shelf.

Treat the bunker as a route rather than one enormous pile. Finish one category family at a time, work in batches, and save detailed manual inspection for the final sweep — that is the most reliable way to make bunker tidy up item placement feel effortless instead of endless.
