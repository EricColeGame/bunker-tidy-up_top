## Core Mechanics: How the Sorting System Actually Works

The heart of bunker tidy up organizing is a simple contract: every item has a logical home, and your job is to figure out the logic faster than you can physically carry things. Nothing enforces it with a countdown, but the room's design nudges you constantly.

### Everything Has a Category, and Categories Have Zones

Dozens of supply categories exist, and the big four — food, medicine, tools, and books — do most of the heavy lifting. A practical way to think about it is that categories are not just labels; they are zones. Once a zone exists, every future decision becomes a yes/no question instead of an open-ended one.

| Category | Typical items | Where it belongs | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Food and rations | Cans, jars, dry goods | Kitchen and pantry shelving | Keeps perishables near prep space; frees deep shelves |
| Medicine | Bandages, bottles, kits | Infirmary or labeled medical shelf | Prevents mixing fragile glass with heavy tools |
| Tools | Wrenches, spare parts, hardware | Workshop or utility racks | Heavy items stay low, reducing awkward re-sorting |
| Books and records | Manuals, folders, paper goods | Archive shelves or desk area | Flat, uniform shapes stack predictably |
| Miscellaneous | Odds, ends, duplicates | Overflow bin until categorized | Stops clutter from blocking real decisions |

### Placement Logic Beats Raw Speed

Each of the 100-plus unique items follows its own placement logic, which is the real puzzle layer. Two objects can belong to the same category and still need different shelf heights, orientations, or neighbors. The fastest players are not the ones who move the most crates per minute — they are the ones who stop picking up an item before they know where it is going.

Three rules that consistently pay off:

1. **Read the room before you touch anything.** A two-minute walkthrough saves ten minutes of re-stacking.
2. **Work zone by zone, not item by item.** Clearing one corner completely is faster than half-finishing four corners.
3. **Leave a landing space.** A temporarily empty shelf near the doorway gives you somewhere to drop unidentified items.

## A Step-by-Step First-Session Workflow

New players tend to grab whatever is closest and improvise. That works for about twenty minutes, then the bunker turns into a wall of unlabeled shelves. Try this order instead.

| Step | What you do | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Survey | Walk the whole bunker and note the natural zones | Prevents you from committing to shelves you will regret |
| 2. Claim anchor shelves | Pick one shelf per major category and never move it | Creates reliable landmarks for every later decision |
| 3. Clear the floor | Move loose supplies into rough category piles | Reduces trip distance and visual noise |
| 4. Sort by size | Within a zone, place large items first, small last | Small items fill gaps instead of blocking them |
| 5. Label mentally | Say the zone name out loud as you place items | Reinforces the rule so you stop second-guessing |
| 6. Re-sweep | Do one fast pass to catch strays and duplicates | Catches 90% of the mistakes you made while tired |

A good bunker tidy up organizing session ends with an empty floor, not with every shelf perfectly arranged. Empty floor means the system is working; perfect shelves are a bonus you can chase later.

## Common Organizing Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Most bunker tidy up organizing mistakes come from impatience rather than bad planning. Here are the ones that show up again and again, plus the fix.

| Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open-ended sorting | You carry an item around for a full minute | Assign every item a category before you pick it up |
| Front-loading small items | Shelves look full but nothing fits | Place large, awkward shapes first and fill gaps last |
| Too many zones | You cannot remember your own system | Cap yourself at five or six core zones |
| Mixing duplicates | Three identical boxes end up in three rooms | Build one overflow shelf for duplicates and revisit it |
| Perfecting early | You re-stack the same shelf five times | Finish a full pass, then refine on a second sweep |
| Playing exhausted | You make placement errors and undo work | Save and quit; the bunker is not going anywhere |

Two extra habits help more than any trick. First, keep the floor clear at all times — it is your workspace, not storage. Second, trust your first system. Rebuilding your category scheme halfway through is the single biggest time sink in the game, and the game will never punish you for a slightly imperfect shelf.

## Bunker Tidy Up Organizing FAQ

**Is Bunker Tidy Up stressful or timed?**
No. There are no timers and no pressure mechanics. You can save at any point and play entirely at your own pace, which is why bunker tidy up organizing appeals to players who want something calm after a long day.

**How long does it take to organize everything?**
The game ships with 17,437 supplies and more than 100 unique item types across dozens of shelves, so a full sweep is a long-haul project rather than a single sitting. Community reports suggest most players break it into short, repeat sessions.

**Do I need a powerful PC to play?**
The listed requirements are on the heavier side for the genre — Windows 10, an Intel Core i7 8700K, 16 GB of RAM, and an RTX 3060, with about 3 GB of storage. The game is also Steam Deck Verified, so handheld play is a viable option.

**What is the best way to start?**
Survey the bunker first, claim one anchor shelf per major category (food, medicine, tools, books), then clear the floor before you fine-tune anything. Structure first, aesthetics second — that order makes bunker tidy up organizing feel effortless instead of endless.

Once your zones are set and the floor is clear, the game becomes something closer to meditation than work. That is the real mechanic: not sorting supplies, but building a system you trust enough to stop thinking about.
