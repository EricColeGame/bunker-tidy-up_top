## Why Shelf Discipline Beats Speed in Bunker Tidy Up

Bunker Tidy Up turns a simple chore into a logistics puzzle: scattered supplies, dozens of shelf categories, and a pair of hands that can only hold so much. It feels manageable until you're standing in a room full of generators, canned food, and gas masks with no clue which pile to touch first. That's the moment good bunker tidy up shelf tips stop being optional and start being the difference between a clean room and three hours of backtracking.

Sprinting from pile to pile feels productive, but a misplaced item turns red on the shelf and adds nothing to your progress. These bunker tidy up shelf tips begin with one idea: decide what you're collecting *before* you start collecting it.

Bunker Tidy Up is a relaxed organization simulation where the entire loop is find, carry, and shelve. You can browse the [Bunker Tidy Up listing on the Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=bunker+tidy+up) to see the scale of the item list before you start. The challenge isn't difficulty — it's volume.

### One rule that overrides every shortcut

A shelf only accepts an item when the category matches. Soda doesn't belong with canned goods, pet food doesn't belong with groceries, and a survival manual doesn't belong with medical supplies.

When you're unsure, check the label before unloading instead of testing shelves by trial and error. That single habit prevents the most common time-waster in the game: carrying an item across the bunker, placing it wrong, picking it back up, and walking it somewhere else.

## Core Shelf Rules: What Actually Counts as Done

Most bunker tidy up shelf tips boil down to sequencing — big objects first, small objects last, and one category per trip. Here's the framework that keeps a messy floor from becoming a messy shelf.

| Sorting Principle | Why It Matters | Recommended Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Correct category | Wrong items don't count toward shelf progress | Read the label before you unload |
| Large items first | Bulky supplies are easy to spot and free up floor space | Clear water containers, generators, and armor early |
| Group identical items | Matching stacks cut repeated trips across the bunker | Gather one item type per run |
| Clear visible piles | Buried supplies hide the small items you'll need later | Remove surface clutter before searching deep |
| Sweep vertically | Supplies appear upstairs, downstairs, and beside furniture | Finish one area completely before changing floors |

Large, visible supplies should always come first. Water containers, generators, body armor, backpacks, and fuel canisters take up the most visual space, so removing them instantly makes the rest of the room readable. They're also easy to identify from across the room, which means fewer wrong pickups and fewer wasted hands.

### Know your item groups before you move

Sort your mental map into broad buckets so you can commit to a route:

- **Survival equipment:** water containers, generators, backpacks, fuel canisters
- **Protection gear:** body armor, helmets, gas masks
- **Weapons:** shotguns, axes, machetes, baseball bats
- **Household supplies:** toilet paper, toothpaste, cleaning products
- **Food and drink:** soda, water, canned food, preserved jars, snacks
- **Animal food:** cat food and dog food, kept on their own shelf
- **Books:** farming, healing, survival, hunting, repair, technology

Carrying capacity shapes everything else. Every trip should complete a thought: *"I'm clearing every water container in this room, then I'm walking to the water shelf."* Random pickups fill your hands before you reach the correct storage area.

Community reports consistently point to one habit that separates efficient runs from chaotic ones: finish a category before switching. If you're holding six canned goods and you spot a helmet, leave the helmet. The detour usually costs more than the pickup saves.

## Upgrade Priorities for Faster Shelving

Upgrades are where the game stops testing your patience and starts rewarding planning. Training your eye to spot categories is the skill; upgrades are the toolkit. Upgrade choices are where most bunker tidy up shelf tips pay off fastest, because the right purchase removes an entire type of wasted movement.

| Upgrade | Main Function | Best Time to Buy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Magnet | Pulls nearby matching items into your hands | When one supply type is scattered across a large room |
| Match Finder | Highlights other items of the same type | Right after Magnet, once you're completing categories |
| Carry Capacity | Lets you hold more items per trip | When your hands fill before a route is done |
| Quick Step | Increases movement speed | Once the layout feels familiar and you cross floors often |
| Right Shelf | Shows where a held item belongs | When category labels keep stumping you |
| Auto Loader | Speeds up repeated shelf interactions | When you're placing large groups of identical items |
| Scatter | Places held items onto shelves already holding matches | When clearing big clusters of the same object |

Magnet is a strong early pick because it pulls nearby items of the same type straight into your hands, which is ideal when a common supply is spread across an open room. Match Finder complements it by revealing other copies of whatever you're holding.

### How to spend points based on your bottleneck

1. **Diagnose the problem.** Are you searching too long, carrying too little, walking too far, or fumbling placements? Buy for that specific frustration.
2. **Unlock Magnet first** when you can see several items of the same type from one spot.
3. **Add Match Finder next** once you're working through categories intentionally. Pause and memorize the highlights before they fade — the effect is temporary.
4. **Increase carry capacity** when your hands fill up before a route is finished. Bigger stacks make every later category faster.
5. **Consider Quick Step** as the bunker becomes familiar and you're constantly crossing between floors.

Avoid spending on abilities with long cooldowns that don't touch your current bottleneck. Jump can help with awkward geometry, but it shouldn't replace a utility upgrade that directly improves sorting.

## Sweep Patterns: Route Planning for a Multi-Level Bunker

A consistent sweep pattern beats raw movement speed almost every time. Supplies sit on lower floors, upper floors, balconies, and beside furniture, so a route that changes every run guarantees something gets missed. The best bunker tidy up shelf tips here are about rhythm, not hustle.

| Route Phase | Target Supplies | Route Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1 | Water containers and generators | Sweep visible upper and lower areas first |
| Phase 2 | Armor, backpacks, and fuel canisters | Repeat the same path while floor space is open |
| Phase 3 | Weapons and protection gear | Check corners, furniture edges, and raised platforms |
| Phase 4 | Medicine and cleaning supplies | Search cleared areas for smaller items |
| Phase 5 | Food, books, and household objects | Finish category by category using matching abilities |

When Match Finder triggers, don't sprint off immediately. Stand still for a beat, scan left to right, and memorize where the duplicates are. A two-second pause often saves three separate trips.

Furniture matters too. Couches, banisters, raised platforms, and shelf edges all block direct paths. If an item is visible but you can't reach it, change your approach angle instead of ramming the same obstacle repeatedly.

| Search Problem | Likely Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One item stays missing | It's buried or behind furniture | Clear nearby clutter and check floor edges |
| A shelf won't complete | An item sits on the wrong category shelf | Read labels and move misplaced supplies |
| The room looks empty | Small items blend into the environment | Use Match Finder or inspect cleared piles |
| A route takes forever | Too many category changes | Complete one item group before switching |

### Use completed shelves as landmarks

"Empty" rooms are rarely empty. Small items blend into the environment once the big supplies are gone, which is why the final stage of a bunker tidy up run is usually the slowest part.

## Tricky Categories and Last-Item Cleanup

Some items read instantly; others demand a second look. Food is the biggest offender because it splits into drinks, groceries, canned vegetables, canned meat and fish, preserved food, sweets and snacks, spices and sauces, and animal food.

| Item Group | Common Examples | Sorting Reminder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Survival equipment | Water containers, generators, backpacks | Usually visible and great for early progress |
| Weapons and protection | Shotguns, axes, bats, armor, gas masks | Keep combat tools away from medical items |
| Household supplies | Toilet paper, toothpaste, cleaning products | Inspect labels when items look alike |
| Food and drinks | Soda, water, cereal, canned food, jars | Match the exact food subcategory |
| Animal food | Cat food and dog food | Place on the dedicated pet food shelf |
| Books and knowledge | Farming, healing, survival, hunting books | Read the shelf description before unloading |

Books deserve their own mention. The same shelf family can include healing, farming, hunting, survival, technology, and repair titles, and each may belong to a different category. Treat every book as a small puzzle until the labels feel familiar.

Cleaning products have the opposite problem: they're small, dull, and easy to walk past. After the large supplies are gone, do a dedicated household sweep rather than hoping you'll notice a bottle of cleaner on the way somewhere else.

### Endgame checklist and priorities

Endgame bunker tidy up shelf tips come down to priority order, not endurance. Use this table when the bunker finally looks "done" but isn't.

| Endgame Task | Priority | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finish nearly complete shelves | High | Fewest searches for the fastest progress |
| Use Magnet on common categories | High | Clears many matching items at once |
| Inspect behind furniture | High | Hidden supplies commonly remain there |
| Recheck red placements | Medium | Misplaced items block shelf completion |
| Search isolated small objects | Medium | Best handled after major clutter is gone |

A reliable finishing routine: walk each floor in the same direction, check behind furniture, and fire abilities whenever they come off cooldown. Completed shelves double as visual anchors, so you can focus only on areas that still hold loose items.

The most reliable bunker tidy up shelf tips are boring on purpose — clear the big stuff, upgrade the tools that reduce searching, finish categories in focused batches, and save the final sweep for hidden small items. Order beats urgency every time.

## FAQ About Bunker Tidy Up Shelf Tips

### What's the fastest way to organize the bunker?

Start with large, high-visibility supplies like water containers, generators, armor, backpacks, and fuel. Then work one category at a time, using Magnet and Match Finder to shrink your search time. These bunker tidy up shelf tips work best when you finish a category before switching to the next one.

### Which upgrades should I unlock first?

Magnet and Match Finder deliver the biggest early gains because they cut searching and picking. Carry Capacity and Quick Step matter more once routes get longer and your hands keep filling up mid-trip.

### Why did my item turn red on the shelf?

Red means the category doesn't match, so the item doesn't count toward that shelf's progress. Pick it up, read the nearby labels, and place it in the most specific matching category instead of forcing it onto the same shelf again.

### How do I find the last missing supply?

Clear clutter along your usual route, check behind furniture and beneath visible piles, then re-sweep each floor in the same order. Match Finder can reveal duplicates of an incomplete category, and knowing the shelf layout well makes missing items far easier to spot.
