## Why Bunker Tidy Up Supplies Sort by Category, Not by Shelf

The bunker rearranges itself between cleanup sessions, so exact shelf positions are unreliable. What stays consistent is the category logic. Canned food sits with other groceries, drinks have their own zone, survival gear occupies a separate section, and hygiene products are grouped together. That pattern survives every reshuffle, which makes category recognition the only skill worth grinding.

Category thinking also solves the biggest time-waster in the game: backtracking. Each round trip across the bunker costs you real sorting time, and a wrong drop costs you double. When you pick up an unfamiliar object, decide its family before you decide its shelf.

- **Food and drinks** — the downstairs grocery zone
- **Survival supplies** — upstairs utility and emergency storage
- **Hygiene and household items** — upstairs bathroom and cleaning shelves
- **Pet care** — the upstairs pet-food area
- **Collectibles** — ducks and similar special objects, saved for last

Player experience suggests that the five-family habit alone eliminates most destination confusion, even before you memorize a single shelf label.

## The Bunker Tidy Up Supplies Map: Downstairs vs. Upstairs

The most practical rule in the game is a two-floor split: food and drink go downstairs, and everything survival-, hygiene-, or pet-related goes upstairs. Mixed piles containing batteries, matches, medicine, and small tools can be routed either direction depending on their category label, so check before you commit.

| Destination area | Common item groups | Sorting priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Downstairs | Canned food, drinks, sauces, spices, crackers | High |
| Upstairs | Survival goods, hygiene items, pet food, household supplies | High |
| Mixed piles | Batteries, matches, medicine, small tools | Medium |
| Special objects | Ducks and other collectibles | Low until the main floor is clear |

Treat that table as a sorting map, not a guarantee. Some objects end up tucked behind furniture, buried inside dense stacks, or partially hidden under other items. If something seems to be missing, scan the surrounding floor before you assume the destination shelf is wrong.

### Category Exceptions to Watch For

The trickiest items are the ones that look like something they are not. Energy bars and energy drinks sit in completely different sections despite nearly identical names. Mayonnaise gets shelved as a sauce instead of with groceries. Always confirm the shelf category before dropping a stack — a two-second check beats a full round trip.

A temporary mental checklist helps:

- **Downstairs food:** canned meals, fruit, vegetables, meat, crackers, sugar, peanut butter, jam, sauces
- **Downstairs drinks:** juice, soda, beer, energy drinks
- **Upstairs survival:** energy bars, medicine, matches, batteries, radios, flashlights
- **Upstairs hygiene:** toothbrushes, toothpaste, shampoo, shower gel, razors, deodorant
- **Upstairs pet care:** cat food, dog food

## The Step-by-Step Bunker Tidy Up Supplies Sorting Route

Random scanning is what turns a two-hour cleanup into a six-hour slog. A route-based approach keeps the bunker readable from start to finish, and it works even when the pile arrangement changes.

1. **Clear the main walking areas.** Start with objects around tables, chairs, major shelves, and the central floor. Open paths improve visibility and stop you from walking past items hidden behind large stacks.
2. **Split downstairs and upstairs items.** Use the category map to divide loose objects into two broad groups, then run dedicated trips for each destination.
3. **Target one item type at a time.** Pick a single object — batteries, matches, dog food, canned fruit — use the item search function, collect a reasonable stack, and return it together.
4. **Lean on abilities for dense piles.** Magnet-style collection is most valuable for tiny, numerous objects like batteries, matches, painkillers, and bandages.
5. **Finish with manual inspection.** When only a few hundred items remain, stop relying on abilities and walk each floor section by hand.

| Cleanup phase | Recommended action | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening pass | Clear open floor areas | Improves movement and visibility |
| Category pass | Move food, drinks, and supplies separately | Cuts destination confusion |
| Search pass | Target one item type at a time | Prevents random scanning |
| Dense-pile pass | Use collection abilities | Saves time on small objects |
| Final pass | Inspect manually | Finds isolated or hidden items |

Large stacks deserve special treatment. Community reports from a 2026 promotional post identified 420 batteries in the game, which makes one-by-one collection a waste of time. Search them out in batches, return the full stack, then check the remaining count before switching categories.

**The efficient route in one line:** open floor first, broad categories second, large stacks third, isolated leftovers last.

## Abilities, Search, and Hidden Bunker Tidy Up Supplies Items

Late-game cleanup demands patience rather than speed, because the objects still on the floor are the ones you already walked past twice. In one recorded session, the final missing painkillers were eventually found underneath a carpet rather than on any shelf or table. That is the whole lesson: a floor can look finished and still hide one object.

| Problem | Best response | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Too many tiny objects | Search and collect in batches | Picking up batteries one at a time |
| Item seems absent | Check under furniture and carpet edges | Assuming it was never spawned |
| Wrong destination | Recheck the category label | Trusting appearance alone |
| Final object remains | Scan floors slowly and methodically | Deciding the room is empty |

The three ability tools have distinct roles. **Item search** is the workhorse — use it whenever you know the item type but cannot spot it in a dense pile. **Magnet abilities** are best reserved for tiny items in large quantities or objects buried behind clutter. **Shelf finders** help early on, but lose value once you know the category layout by heart, so stop leaning on them around the midpoint of a cleanup.

A few habits make the whole system more dependable:

- Search before you walk every pile
- Keep the selected item visible while scanning
- Check the floor directly beneath your character
- Inspect carpet edges, corners, and furniture undersides
- Revisit dense piles after the surrounding area is clear
- Use completion counts to confirm a category is actually finished

For collectible ducks, wait until the main storage categories are under control. Community reports note that duck audio can be noticeably louder than other game sounds, so lowering your overall volume before the final duck hunt makes the process far more comfortable.

**Q: What are the main bunker tidy up supplies destination groups?**

Downstairs handles food and drinks, while upstairs takes survival supplies, hygiene products, pet food, and household essentials. Batteries, matches, radios, and medicine typically come out of mixed utility piles.

**Q: Where do energy drinks and energy bars go?**

Energy drinks belong with the drink group downstairs. Energy bars are classified as survival supplies upstairs. Keeping those two separate prevents repeat sorting errors.

**Q: What is the fastest way to clear batteries?**

Use the item search function, collect batteries in manageable batches, and switch to a magnet-style ability when the pile becomes hard to scan. Given how large the battery group is, single-item pickup is never worth it.

**Q: What should I do when one item is missing?**

Search carpet edges, underneath furniture, corners, and dense piles. A final object is often partially hidden rather than sitting visibly on a shelf.

The central strategy never changes: learn the category destinations, work in batches, and save detailed manual inspection for the last sweep. Treat the bunker as a route rather than one enormous pile, and a 17,000-item cleanup stops feeling impossible.
