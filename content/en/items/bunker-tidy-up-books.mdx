## Why Books Deserve Their Own Sorting Route

You clear a corridor, turn a corner, and there it is: a shoulder-high drift of paperbacks, repair manuals, and waterlogged journals. Bunker Tidy Up books are one of the game's named supply categories, and they are also the easiest category to misplace. Sorting Bunker Tidy Up books properly keeps shelves readable, protects your walking routes, and stops the same stack from migrating across the bunker three separate times.

Books behave differently from food or medicine. Cans stack, bandages bundle, and tools have obvious silhouettes — but volumes are flat, similar in profile, and nearly identical at a glance. Community reports consistently flag the mixed book pile as the spot where sorting runs stall, because you keep stopping to check a cover before deciding where it goes. A dedicated book route fixes that: grab them in bulk, carry them to one destination, finish the category in a single loop.

This guide covers category rules, shelf zones, a batch routine for large piles, and the skills worth prioritizing. It also flags the mistakes that quietly undo an otherwise clean bunker.

## Category Rules: What Actually Counts as a Book

The organizing principle in Bunker Tidy Up is function, not appearance. A thin hardcover and a thick manual belong together if both exist to be read, while a clipboard that happens to be flat belongs with tools or office supplies. When an item is ambiguous, compare it with nearby objects and ask what the item is *for* before asking what it looks like.

Use the item's reading or reference purpose as your deciding factor, then commit to that call for the rest of the run.

| Reading item | Function cue | Where it belongs | Common trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leisure reading | Fiction, stories, entertainment | Main bookcase, easy-reach shelf | Treated as decor and left out on tables |
| Reference and instruction | Manuals, guides, how-to material | Bookcase near the workshop or workbench | Split between tools and books across two rooms |
| Logs and notebooks | Written records, notes, journals | Bookcase archive shelf, deeper storage | Dropped into a tools crate because of size |
| Bound periodicals | Magazines, pamphlets, loose pages | Bookcase, bundled with a tie or clip | Left loose and scattered into other piles |
| Damaged copies | Torn, stained, waterlogged | Same shelf, tucked at the back | Thrown away instead of shelved, breaking category counts |

The last row matters more than it looks. Consistency beats perfection: if damaged books stay with books, your shelf log stays accurate and you stop second-guessing yourself on later passes.

## Building a Book Zone Map

Before you pick up a single volume, decide where books live. Planning the shelf first is the single biggest time saver in the whole category, because it eliminates the "I'll figure it out when I get there" pause that turns a two-minute trip into a ten-minute detour.

A simple three-tier zone map works for most bunkers. Reserve the middle height for the volumes you handle most, push low-value duplicates and damaged copies deep, and keep one clearly marked staging space for unsorted paper.

| Zone | Contents | Shelving height | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary bookcase | Everyday reading and reference | Middle shelf, arm level | Fastest access for the items you revisit |
| Secondary shelf | Duplicates, damaged copies, rare titles | Upper or lower shelf | Keeps clutter off the main shelf without losing the category |
| Deep storage rack | Overflow waiting for a permanent home | Far wall or back room | Absorbs bulk finds without breaking your layout |
| Staging crate | Unsorted paper straight off the floor | Near the room entrance | Stops loose pages from drifting into other categories |

Give every zone one purpose and leave visible gaps between categories. A gap is free signage — you can confirm a shelf is correct from across the room without walking over to inspect the spines.

Once the map is set, books become the fastest category to process, and you can spend the saved time on messier groups like tools and mixed consumables.

## The Batch Routine for Large Book Piles

When a room dumps dozens of reading items at once, resist the urge to place each one perfectly on the first pass. Broad sorting first, detail second. That is the same principle the game's own progression advice uses for large supply drops, and it applies double to flat, hard-to-tell-apart objects.

| Step | Action | Time saved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sweep the section and gather every paper item into one pile | Eliminates repeated trips to distant corners |
| 2 | Separate into three rough stacks: reading, reference, records | Prevents spine-by-spine decision fatigue |
| 3 | Carry each stack to its zone in one haul using full capacity | Cuts walking distance dramatically |
| 4 | Place items on the shelf, then align and space them | Turns placement into a quick finishing pass |
| 5 | Walk the room again for stragglers behind shelves and boxes | Catches hidden objects before you move on |

Hidden objects are a core part of the game, and paper items are notorious for tucking behind shelf backs, under crates, and in corner shadows. A deliberate second sweep of any room that produced books is worth the thirty seconds it costs.

Finish the area completely before opening a new one. Half-sorted rooms are where categories bleed into each other, and a book that ends up in a tools area will cost you two future trips instead of one.

## Skills and Upgrades That Speed Up Book Work

Skill points are limited, so spend them where books actually slow you down. Book runs are usually about volume and distance rather than precision, which makes carrying improvements unusually valuable here.

| Skill | Effect on book sorting | Priority for book-heavy runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity | Fewer trips between piles and the bookcase | High early, when paper is spread across the bunker |
| Carry speed | Shorter travel time on long hauls to deep storage | High once shelves are far apart |
| Cleaning efficiency | Faster completion of remaining cleanup work | Best after carrying and movement are handled |

If your bunker's bookcase sits on the opposite side of the map from where you find most paper, prioritize carry speed before capacity. If piles are near the shelf but enormous, flip that order. The right sequence depends entirely on your layout, so observe your own runs for a few minutes before spending.

There is no upgrade that replaces a good zone map. Skills shorten the work you already planned; they cannot fix a shelf with no assigned purpose.

## Five Book-Sorting Mistakes That Undo Your Progress

- **Sorting books before clearing walkways.** Loose paper in a doorway blocks the exact route you need for every other category. Open the main paths first.
- **Shelving by size instead of function.** Matching spines look tidy but break down the moment a new title arrives. Function-based groups survive new supply drops.
- **Creating a new group for one odd item.** Move the outlier into an existing category rather than founding a shelf for a single volume.
- **Filling every shelf to the edge.** Leave headroom for the next batch so you never have to relocate a whole category.
- **Skipping the maintenance pass.** New items should go straight to their assigned zone. A short, repeated check keeps the bunker from sliding back into chaos.

Each of these costs minutes, not hours — but they compound across a bunker holding tens of thousands of supplies. Player experience suggests the maintenance habit alone saves the most time over a full completion run.

## Keeping the Library Readable Long Term

A finished book section should be self-explanatory. Stand at the entrance to the room and try to identify each shelf's purpose from where you are. If you cannot, the layout needs one more refinement pass.

Label-free cues work well here: consistent gaps, a single wall of reference material, and damaged copies always at the back. These small conventions mean you can sort a fresh pile of paper without thinking, and that is the real goal — a bunker where tidying takes effort only at the start of a session, not in the middle of one.

## FAQ

**What counts as a book in Bunker Tidy Up?**
Anything whose main purpose is reading or reference: fiction, manuals, notebooks, bound periodicals, and loose paper bundles. Judge by function rather than shape, and keep damaged copies on the same shelf as intact ones so your counts stay consistent.

**Should I sort books before or after tools?**
Clear your main walkways first, then process books. Paper spreads quickly and hides in corners, so moving it early prevents loose pages from contaminating tool areas later in the run.

**How do I keep a large pile from spreading again?**
Assign one staging crate near the room entrance, shelf items in the same session you gather them, and return new finds to their zone immediately. Consistent placement does more than any single upgrade.

**Do Bunker Tidy Up books count toward achievements?**
Cleanup milestones and completion objectives track your overall organization progress, so finishing the book category contributes to those goals. Check the in-game achievement list for the exact objectives in your build.
