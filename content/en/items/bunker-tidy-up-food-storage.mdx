## Bunker Tidy Up Food Storage Categories: What Goes Where

Most sorting mistakes happen on borderline items, not on obvious ones. A can of soup is never confusing. A cooking pot, a pack of vitamins, or a sealed bag of garden seeds will stall a run for minutes at a time if you have not decided in advance where those items live.

The reliable rule is function over appearance. Ask what the object is *used for*, not what it looks like or where you found it. A canteen looks like a tool, but it belongs with provisions because it supports eating and drinking. A first-aid tin with a food-style label still belongs in medical storage, because its purpose is treatment.

| Borderline Item | Food Zone? | Correct Home | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canned soup or stew | Yes | Main provision shelf | Edible without preparation |
| Water bottles and canteens | Yes | Drink shelf within the food zone | Consumed like food |
| Cooking pot or pan | No | Kitchen shelf beside the food zone | A tool, but it belongs near prep space |
| Vitamins and supplements | No | Medical storage | Health purpose overrides packaging |
| Cleaning chemicals | No | Utility shelf far from provisions | Keeps consumables visually separate |
| Seeds and garden packs | Sometimes | Long-term provision shelf | Future food, often grouped as misc |

Once these rulings are locked in, sorting speeds up noticeably. You stop re-deciding and start moving.

## A Step-by-Step Bunker Tidy Up Food Storage Workflow

The fastest runs follow the same rhythm: clear a path, build a staging pile, sort broad categories, then refine placement. Skipping straight to perfect shelf alignment is the single biggest time sink for new players, because you rearrange the same crate three times.

| Step | Focus | What "Done" Looks Like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Survey | Walk the food rooms without picking anything up | You know roughly how many items are loose |
| 2. Clear routes | Move obstacles out of doorways and corridors | Main walkways are open |
| 3. Stage | Drop all food items in one temporary pile | Loose food is no longer scattered |
| 4. Broad sort | Split the pile into cans, dry goods, drinks, and misc | Four to six rough groups exist |
| 5. Place | Move each group to its assigned shelf | Every group has a home |
| 6. Refine | Align, space, and add gaps for future items | Shelves look consistent and roomy |

Work one room at a time and finish it before moving on. Carrying items toward their destination on the way to your next pickup — rather than making dedicated trips — cuts travel time dramatically across a full run.

If a large delivery lands mid-sort, do not integrate it immediately. Batch it into the same broad groups and fold it in after the current room is finished. Interrupting a placement pass to chase new items is how zones fall apart.

## Shelf Planning: Rules for a Provision Zone That Scales

Shelf planning decides whether your Bunker Tidy Up food storage stays clean for the rest of the run or collapses back into clutter within an hour. Decide the layout before you pick up a single crate, and leave breathing room for items you have not found yet.

| Zone | Height | Contents | Rule of Thumb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy shelf | Waist to chest | Cans, jars, dense items | Heavy items never go above shoulder height |
| Drink shelf | Waist height | Bottles and canteens | Keep fragile containers off the top shelf |
| Dry goods shelf | Knee to waist | Sacks, boxes, pouches | Leave vertical gaps for soft-sided items |
| Quick grab shelf | Chest to eye level | Rations, everyday snacks | Most-used items sit in the easiest reach |
| Overflow shelf | Top or deep corner | Duplicates and bulk extras | Only fill after the main zones are complete |
| Kitchen edge | Beside the food zone | Pots, utensils, prep tools | Keeps tools close without mixing categories |

Three habits keep the plan intact:

- **Reserve space on purpose.** Never fill a shelf to the edge; new food items arrive constantly.
- **Keep categories visually separate.** Even a small gap between groups prevents mixing.
- **Protect walkways.** Nothing loose in doorways, corridors, or narrow passages, no matter how temporary.

Your skill choices change how long the food phase takes. Food runs are travel-heavy, because provisions are spread across multiple rooms and the nearest shelf is rarely close by.

| Skill | What It Changes | Why It Matters for Food Runs | Best Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Cleaning Efficiency | Speeds up late cleanup work | Helps finish the last messy corners of the shelter | After carrying and movement feel comfortable |

Common mistakes that undo good sorting:

- Placing food items "temporarily" in a hallway, then never moving them.
- Rearranging one shelf repeatedly before the first pass is complete.
- Mixing drinks into the general provision shelf instead of a fixed drink zone.
- Ignoring hidden objects behind crates, which then get relocated and lost.
- Creating new categories for single items instead of folding them into an existing group.

Maintenance is the final piece. After the main cleanup ends, return every new item to its assigned area immediately, and repeat a quick route check whenever you rearrange a shelf. A short maintenance pass at the end of each session prevents the bunker from sliding back into its original state.

## FAQ

**How many supplies are there in Bunker Tidy Up?**

Community reports count 17,437 supplies across the entire shelter, spanning food, medicine, tools, books, and miscellaneous items. Food is one of the largest groups, which is why a dedicated Bunker Tidy Up food storage plan saves so much time.

**Should I sort food first or medicine first?**

Start with food. It is the biggest and most scattered category, and clearing it opens the main walkways so medicine, tools, and books become much faster to handle afterward.

**Where do bottled water and canteens belong?**

Keep them inside the food zone on a dedicated drink shelf at waist height. They are consumed like food, but separating them from cans and dry goods prevents fragile containers from being crushed.

**How do I stop my food shelves from getting messy again?**

Set the layout before you start placing items, leave unused space on every shelf, and process new deliveries in batches. Return each new item to its assigned spot right away instead of dropping it wherever there is room.
