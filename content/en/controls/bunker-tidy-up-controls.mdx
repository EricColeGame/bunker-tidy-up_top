## Keyboard and Mouse vs. Controller: Which Sorts Faster?

Player experience suggests that neither input method is objectively better — they simply suit different phases of the cleanup. Mouse aiming makes it easier to inspect corners and click precise shelf slots, while a controller is more comfortable for the long, repetitive walk-and-carry loops that dominate mid-game.

| Criteria | Keyboard & Mouse | Controller | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Camera precision | High — quick flicks to inspect shelves | Moderate — steady but slower to fine-tune | Spotting hidden objects |
| Long session comfort | Depends on desk setup | Generally easier on the hands | Multi-hour organizing marathons |
| Placement accuracy | Very fine control | Slightly looser | Aligning items on shelves |
| Rebinding flexibility | Wide | Limited by available buttons | Players with specific habits |
| Switching held items | Fast if bound near movement keys | Fast on shoulder buttons | Bulk carrying runs |

A practical compromise many players report using: keyboard and mouse for the initial survey and the final alignment pass, then controller for the bulk sorting in between. There is no penalty for swapping mid-session, so experiment rather than forcing one setup to do everything.

## How to Remap and Troubleshoot Your Bunker Tidy Up Controls

Because the game leans on a small command set, a single awkward binding can define your whole session. Remapping takes a couple of minutes and pays for itself almost immediately.

1. **Open the settings menu** from the pause screen or the main menu.
2. **Find the controls or bindings section** and read through every listed command before changing anything.
3. **Rebind one command at a time**, then test it in a low-stakes area rather than mid-route with a full load.
4. **Keep related commands together** — for example, place pickup and drop on inputs your hand can reach without repositioning.
5. **Test controller options separately** if you switch devices, since keyboard and controller bindings are usually stored independently.
6. **Re-check bindings after any update**, because control and accessibility adjustments are a common patch note category.

| Setting Area | What to Check | Why It Helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bindings | Every action mapped to a comfortable input | Removes awkward reaches during long sessions |
| Sensitivity / camera | Look speed that suits your shelf-inspection style | Faster surveys without overshooting |
| Accessibility options | Toggles for prompts, readability, and comfort | Keeps interaction prompts easy to read |
| Controller options | Dead zones and button layout | Smoother movement on worn sticks |

If a command stops responding, the cause is usually one of three things: the binding was overwritten, a controller profile loaded incorrectly, or the game is waiting on an interaction prompt that has not appeared yet. Step back from the object and approach it again before assuming something is broken.

## Control Habits That Make Big Cleanup Runs Faster

Knowing the bindings is not the same as using them well. These habits come from community reports and general organizing practice, and they work with any input device.

| Habit | What It Does | Best Used When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full look pass before touching anything | Reveals hidden supplies and labels the room's categories | Entering a new section |
| One-directional routes | Cuts backtracking between pickup and storage | Walking long corridors |
| Load then place, never drip-feed | Uses item switching instead of repeated trips | Carrying capacity is upgraded |
| Clear walkways first | Prevents dropped items from blocking doors | Start of every session |
| Batch sorting for large drops | Groups broad categories before fine placement | New supplies arrive in bulk |

- **Bind item switching within thumb or finger reach.** Cycling your carried load mid-route is the single biggest time saver.
- **Use the movement keys for short corrections and the camera for aiming.** Fighting both at once causes most accidental drops.
- **Drop into a staging zone, not a shelf, when you are unsure.** Refine placement in a second pass.
- **Finish one storage area before opening another.** Consistent placement means fewer revisits.

The underlying philosophy is simple: bunker tidy up controls should serve your route, not the other way around. If a binding forces you to stop walking to change items, it is costing you time on every single trip.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I change the bunker tidy up controls on console-style controllers?**
Yes. Controller bindings, dead zones, and layout options are typically available in the same settings area as keyboard and mouse bindings. Check the controller section specifically, since profiles are often stored separately from keyboard mappings.

**What is the most important bunker tidy up controls binding to change?**
Item switching. It is the command you use most often during bulk carrying, and a comfortable switch input removes the most wasted seconds per session.

**Why does my interact key sometimes do nothing?**
Usually because the interaction prompt has not registered. Step back, face the object directly, and approach again. If it persists across several items, verify your bindings have not been overwritten.

**Do control settings affect how fast I can organize the bunker?**
They affect efficiency rather than raw speed. Comfortable bindings reduce misclicks, accidental drops, and menu visits, which adds up considerably across thousands of items.
