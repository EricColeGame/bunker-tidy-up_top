## Why Bunker Tidy Up PC Controls Matter More Than You Think

Bunker Tidy Up is a game about 17,437 scattered supplies and exactly one pair of hands — yours. Every shelf you fill is built from hundreds of tiny inputs: grab, carry, place, adjust, repeat. If your Bunker Tidy Up PC controls feel clumsy in the first hour, they will feel unbearable by hour ten.

That's not a small problem. The game ships with more than 100 unique items spread across dozens of categories such as food, medicine, tools, and books, and it deliberately removes pressure: no timers, no fail states, no rush. The only thing that can make a session feel bad is friction between you and the keyboard. Fix that, and the rest of the game becomes the calm, meditative shelf-stacking experience it was designed to be.

The good news is that a tidy-up game rewards optimization more than almost any other genre. Sorting is a repetition engine, and small control wins compound fast.

### The math behind "one more second"

| Friction point | Extra time per item | Cost across 500 items |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slow grab or release | ~0.5 s | ~4 minutes |
| Re-aiming the camera | ~1 s | ~8 minutes |
| Hunting for the right shelf | ~3 s | ~25 minutes |
| Fumbling a menu or hotbar | ~2 s | ~17 minutes |

Those are rough player-side estimates, not developer figures — but they illustrate the point. Cleaning up half a second here and there can hand you back the better part of an hour. Community reports on the [official Steam store page for Bunker Tidy Up](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5212940/) back this up: at the time of writing the game sits at a "Very Positive" rating with roughly 88% of several hundred reviews recommending it, and the most common praise is exactly the thing controls protect — a relaxing, unhurried loop.

## Setting Up Your Bunker Tidy Up PC Controls

You don't need a custom keymap to enjoy the game, but ten minutes in the options menu pays for itself immediately.

### Step 1: Locate the input settings

Open the settings or options menu from the main menu or the in-game pause screen, then look for a Controls, Input, or Key Bindings tab. The interface and subtitles are localized into 15 languages, so the labels will match whatever language your Steam client and game are set to. If the exact tab names differ from what you expect, scan for the tab that lists movement and interaction actions — that's your target.

### Step 2: Audit the actions you repeat most

Most of your playtime will be spent on a handful of actions. Prioritize those, and don't bother optimizing anything you touch once an hour.

| Action to look for | Why it matters | What to aim for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary interact / grab | Used thousands of times per session | A key you can tap without shifting your hand off movement |
| Secondary or alternate interact | Rotation, dropping, or fine placement | Adjacent to primary, never a stretch |
| Movement (forward/back/strafe) | Constant, especially in narrow corridors | Standard WASD or your existing muscle memory |
| Crouch or lower stance | Reaching low shelves without camera gymnastics | A modifier-free key near your left pinky |
| Camera sensitivity | Aiming at shelf slots precisely | Lower than your shooter settings |
| Pause / menu | Checking objectives and progress | Somewhere you can't hit by accident |
| Save | Manual safety net | Anywhere comfortable — Save Anytime is supported |

### Step 3: Tune sensitivity before you tune anything else

A first-person organizing game is really a precision game. You're not flicking at enemies; you're placing a small box onto a small slot. That means most players should start lower than their typical first-person sensitivity and raise it only if turning around feels sluggish. If the game exposes an invert-Y toggle and you're used to it, set it now — switching mid-playthrough is painful.

### Step 4: Decide on a controller

The store page lists both Xbox and PlayStation controller support, so a gamepad is a legitimate first choice. If you already own one, try it for a full session before committing to keyboard and mouse, or vice versa.

## Keyboard and Mouse vs Controller vs Steam Deck

There's no universal winner here — it depends on how you play and where you sit.

| Setup | Precision for placing items | Comfort over long sessions | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keyboard + mouse | Highest — mouse aim maps directly to cursor placement | Good, assuming a decent desk and chair | Detail-obsessed players who want fast, exact shelf placement |
| Xbox / PlayStation controller | Good, with analog camera control | Excellent for couch or recliner play | Long, relaxed sessions where you lean back |
| Steam Deck (Verified) | Good — trackpads help with fine aiming | Excellent for portable play | Playing in short bursts or away from your desk |
| Laptop trackpad | Weakest for fine placement | Poor for multi-hour sessions | Emergencies only |

A few practical notes from player experience:

- **Steam Deck owners** get an official Verified rating, which means the default control layout, text size, and performance are considered playable without tinkering. The trackpad is a real advantage for precise placement if thumbsticks feel twitchy.
- **Controller users** should expect slightly slower sorting speed but noticeably less hand fatigue on multi-hour sessions.
- **Keyboard and mouse users** should consider a larger mousepad. Long lateral camera sweeps across a bunker corridor eat desk space fast.
- **Steam Cloud** support means your progress and settings follow you between a desktop and a Deck, so you don't have to rebuild your input preferences on every device. Family Sharing also applies, which is handy if someone else in the house wants their own save.

## Tidying Tips That Reward Good Controls

Controls only take you so far. The other half of the equation is process. These habits pair well with a tuned input setup.

1. **Work in zones, not in categories.** Pick one room or shelf bank and finish it completely before moving on. Constant cross-bunker trips are the single biggest source of wasted camera movement.
2. **Batch by category on the floor first.** Pile food together, medicine together, tools together, then shelve each pile. Your hands stay in a rhythm instead of switching mental modes every ten seconds.
3. **Keep your camera at shelf height.** Set it once per aisle and only adjust vertically when you change shelf rows. Constant re-aiming is what burns out your wrist.
4. **Use one hand for carry, one for camera.** A stable division of labor makes long sessions feel automatic.
5. **Do a fast first pass, then a detail pass.** Rough placement first, then fine-tune. Perfectionism on pass one is how a relaxing game becomes a chore.
6. **Save before big reorganizations.** Since Save Anytime is supported, there's no reason to gamble an hour of sorting on a risky reshuffle.

| Supply category | Suggested placement logic |
| --- | --- |
| Food | Near the entrance or living areas — most frequently "used" items go where you'd reach first |
| Medicine | Together, in one clearly marked block, away from heavy tools |
| Tools | Low shelves, so heaviest items need the least lifting and awkward reaching |
| Books | High shelves and tight rows — uniform shapes stack efficiently |
| Mixed / uncategorized | A single "sorting table" area near the door, emptied every few minutes |

Those are organizational suggestions rather than game rules — placement logic varies per item — but the principle holds: decide once, then stop deciding.

## FAQ

**Are Bunker Tidy Up PC controls rebindable?**
Most PC organizing sims of this type let you remap at least the core interaction keys, and the settings menu is the first place to check. Look for a Controls or Key Bindings tab. If a specific action isn't rebindable in your build, remap your mouse's side buttons through your mouse software instead — it achieves the same result without touching the game files.

**Can I play Bunker Tidy Up PC with a controller or on Steam Deck?**
Yes. Xbox and PlayStation controllers are listed as supported inputs, and the game carries an official Steam Deck Verified rating, meaning the default layout and text are considered playable out of the box. Steam Cloud syncs your settings between devices.

**Is there any time pressure that makes a slow control setup a problem?**
No. The game explicitly has no timers and is described as playable at your own pace, with no quick time events. Your control setup affects comfort and speed, never your ability to succeed.

**What should I change first if the controls feel off?**
Lower your camera sensitivity, then move your primary interact key under a finger that never has to leave movement. Those two changes fix the majority of "this feels bad" complaints in first-person organizing games, and both take under five minutes in the settings menu.

Sorting 17,437 supplies is a marathon, not a sprint. Get your Bunker Tidy Up PC controls comfortable before you commit to a long session, and the bunker will practically tidy itself.
