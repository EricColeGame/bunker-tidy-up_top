## The Four-Action Loop Behind Every Cleanup Run

Every successful sorting route in Bunker Tidy Up is the same loop repeated a few thousand times. Understanding why each step exists makes the controls feel intuitive instead of mechanical.

**Move and look.** Movement gets you between the clutter and the storage zones, while the look input lets you scan shelves for misfiled items. Because supplies are deliberately tucked behind shelves, boxes, and corners, you should sweep a room visually before you start lifting anything.

**Pick up and interact.** When you approach a movable item, an interaction prompt appears. That prompt is your confirmation that the game recognizes the object as interactive — if you press the interact input and nothing happens, the item is usually scenery or already assigned to a container.

**Place and drop.** Placement is the step where careless players damage their own layout. Position the item before confirming, and avoid dumping it in doorways or narrow passages.

**Switch held item.** Once you upgrade carrying capacity, you will routinely hold several supplies at once. Switching between them lets you finish a shelf without walking back to your staging pile.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scan the room | Look | Sweep shelves before lifting | Hidden supplies stay buried |
| Approach item | Move | Plan a route, not a straight line | Endless backtracking |
| Grab supply | Interact | Confirm the prompt appears | Wasted inputs on scenery |
| Set it down | Place | Align before confirming | Blocked walkways, mixed categories |
| Rotate carried items | Item switch | Empty your hands shelf by shelf | Extra trips across the bunker |

## How to Rebind Your Keys and What to Change First

Player experience suggests that the default layout is workable, but the settings menu exists for a reason: comfort matters when a single session can involve hundreds of pickups. Here is the practical order to work through when tuning your setup.

| Priority | Setting to Review | Why It Helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Interact / pick up | Your most-used input by far — put it where your index finger rests naturally |
| 2 | Place / drop | Used almost as often as interact; keep it distinct so you never mix the two up |
| 3 | Item switch | Reaching across the keyboard mid-shelf wastes time and breaks your rhythm |
| 4 | Menu / settings | Low frequency, but should never be adjacent to a sorting input |
| 5 | Sensitivity and look controls | Smoother camera movement makes shelf scanning less tedious |

A good rule of thumb: separate your interact and place inputs by enough distance that a slip of the hand cannot trigger the wrong one, but keep both within a single comfortable reach. Also confirm your item-switch input before you invest heavily in the Capacity skill — the two work as a pair.

If a binding stops responding, check whether a context prompt is active. Bunker Tidy Up uses context-sensitive interactions, so the same input can behave differently depending on what you are looking at.

## Control Habits That Speed Up Large Cleanups

Knowing the inputs is half the job. The other half is using them in an order that respects how the game's systems work. The following habits come from general organization-game practice and community tips for large supply volumes.

- **Bind for rhythm, not for reach.** Sorting is a repeated motion, so an input you can hit without stretching beats a "logical" layout every time.
- **Clear a section before switching tools.** Finish the shelf in front of you before rotating to a new held item.
- **Use the look input as a search tool.** Sweep low edges, shelf undersides, and corners where hidden objects tend to sit.
- **Keep placement deliberate.** One careful placement saves two later corrections, especially around doors and corridors.
- **Batch large supply drops.** Group items into broad categories with the switch input before doing detailed placement.

| --- | --- | --- |

| Move | Carry Speed | Shorter travel time on long routes |

| Item switch | Capacity | Keeps a full load organized mid-route |

The pairing is straightforward: Capacity reduces how often you walk, Carry Speed reduces how long each walk takes, and Cleaning Efficiency shortens the finishing work. All three make your control inputs count for more, which is exactly what you want from the bunker tidy up keyboard controls over a long play session.

## FAQ

**What are the default Bunker Tidy Up keyboard controls?**
The game groups inputs into categories — move, look, pick up/interact, place/drop, switch held item, and menu — and the exact bindings are visible in the settings menu. Instead of memorizing a chart, open the controls screen once and confirm your interact and place inputs first, since those two drive almost every action.

**Can I rebind the Bunker Tidy Up keyboard controls?**
Yes. Bindings are adjustable in the settings menu, and the game also offers controller configuration options. Rebinding is worth doing early, before you have organized a large section, so your muscle memory develops around the layout you will actually use.

**Do I need a controller to play Bunker Tidy Up?**
No. Keyboard and mouse covers the full action set, and many players consider it the more precise option for aligning items on shelves. A controller is a comfort choice rather than a requirement.

**Why is my interact input not picking something up?**
Most of the time, the object simply is not interactive. Wait for the on-screen prompt to appear before pressing the input. If a prompt is showing and the input still fails, check your bindings — an accidental rebind is a common cause.
