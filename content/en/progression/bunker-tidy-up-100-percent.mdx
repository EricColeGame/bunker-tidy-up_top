## The Four Phases of a Bunker Tidy Up 100 Percent Run

The game's promotional description puts the bunker at roughly 17,437 sortable items, which immediately tells you what kind of project this is: a long-haul organization sim, not a quick afternoon mission. The item spread covers food, drinks, household products, survival equipment, medical supplies, books, weapons, and plenty more.

Player experience suggests progress follows a fairly predictable curve. The opening hours move quickly because large objects are easy to spot, then everything slows down once only small, similar-looking objects remain. Understanding which phase you're in keeps you from using the wrong tool at the wrong time.

## Upgrade Priority: Capacity First, Speed Later

Upgrades are the single biggest lever on your completion time. Early points should reduce the number of trips you take. Later points should reduce the amount of time you spend searching. Players who flip that order end up walking the same corridors twice as often.

Carrying capacity and hand storage matter most in the first hour, because every return trip to a shelf is pure overhead. Once you can move a full category in one or two loads, Magnetism and item-finding tools start paying for themselves immediately.

| Upgrade | Priority | Best time to buy | What it really saves you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pickup / carry capacity | Highest | First few points | Dozens of shelf return trips |
| Stack handling | High | Early to mid | Time spent shuffling identical copies |
| Movement speed | Medium | Early or mid | Long floor-to-floor walks |
| Magnetism | High | Mid-game | Manual pickup time on scattered groups |
| Item finder | High | Mid to late | Hours of blind searching for rare copies |
| Ultimate deposit | Medium-high | Late game | Sorting time on mixed inventories |

### What to skip early

Do not sink your first points into jump height or cosmetic movement perks. Faster movement cannot compensate for hobbling back to the shelves with a nearly empty inventory, and vertical mobility rarely solves the actual bottleneck, which is item identification. Save those quality-of-life purchases for after capacity, Magnetism, and the finder are online.

Community reports also warn against over-upgrading one ability to the exclusion of others. A balanced kit — moderate capacity, working Magnetism, functional finder, reliable Ultimate — outperforms a single maxed-out stat almost every time.

## A Repeatable Sorting Route, Step by Step

1. **Do one empty-handed lap.** Walk both floors with nothing in your hands and note where the major zones sit: drinks, groceries, snacks, spices and sauces, canned food, pet food, household supplies, survival gear, medicine, books, and weapons.
2. **Clear large objects first.** Water barrels, fuel canisters, backpacks, generators, fire extinguishers, and boxes have unmistakable silhouettes. They build early experience and open up floor space.
3. **Build two-category routes.** Pair item types whose shelves sit near each other so a single trip covers two needs instead of one.
4. **Deposit often, but not obsessively.** Empty a full inventory at natural break points rather than running back after every third item.
5. **Fire abilities at clusters, not leftovers.** Magnetism and the Ultimate deposit are worth far more on a dense group of twenty matching items than on the last two stragglers.
6. **Switch to precision mode.** Once a category is nearly done, hold one copy of the item and let the finder guide you instead of relying on memory.

| Item group | Useful visual clue | Handling note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drinks | Bottles, cans, cartons | Batch with water products when shelves are close |
| Canned food | Uniform metal cylinders | Easy to spot in piles, easy to miss alone |
| Pet food | Bags and cans | Keep dog and cat products separated |
| Books | Flat rectangles and visible spines | Good filler item for mid-game trips |
| Medical supplies | White kits, bandages, blister packs | The finder ability shines here |
| Household goods | Cleaning bottles, boxes, tubes | Check upper shelves and corners carefully |
| Survival gear | Backpacks, extinguishers, tools | Clear early while silhouettes are obvious |

## Late-Game Cleanup: Chasing the Last Hidden Items

The final stretch is where most bunker tidy up 100 percent attempts stall. Large categories are finished, and what's left are hundreds of similar small objects — cans, jars, batteries, matches, toothpaste, food packets — that visually blend into the floor and shelving.

The fix is discipline, not speed. Pick a category with a manageable remaining count, finish it completely, then move on. Switching constantly between item types makes it impossible to remember what you've already checked and guarantees repeat searches.

| Late-game problem | Practical solution |
| --- | --- |
| Small items vanish visually | Search by shape, color, and shelf category rather than by name |
| One or two copies remain | Hold a matching item and trigger the finder |
| Inventory fills instantly | Deposit more often and raise hand capacity |
| Abilities feel wasted | Save them for dense clusters of remaining copies |
| An item won't place anywhere | Check the category indicator before guessing |
| Progress appears frozen | Re-check the lower floor and previously cleared routes |

### Final completion checklist

- Walk both bunker floors and every accessible corner
- Finish low-count categories by hand instead of with abilities
- Use Magnetism only on large repeated groups
- Keep one item in hand whenever you trigger the finder
- Spend the Ultimate deposit on a full or mixed load
- Confirm no shelf category has an unfinished stack

The last 10 percent deserves its own plan. Abilities that carried you through the middle of the run are often wasted at the end, so slow down, scan methodically, and treat each remaining category as a small, self-contained job.

## FAQ

**How long does bunker tidy up 100 percent completion take?**
Most players should budget several hours. One widely shared playthrough reference reaches the finish in roughly four and a half hours, but your time depends heavily on upgrade choices, how quickly you learn the shelf layout, and how methodically you handle the final cleanup.

**Which upgrade should I buy first?**
Carry capacity. It's the safest early investment because it makes every single trip more productive. Follow it with stack handling, then Magnetism, then item finding, and finally the Ultimate deposit once your inventories are consistently full.

**Is it better to sort one category at a time?**
Late in the run, absolutely. Category-focused sorting makes remaining counts easy to track and stops you from re-searching rooms you already cleared. Early on, you can afford to be looser and grab whatever large objects are nearby.

**What should I do when only a few items remain?**
Hold one copy of the missing item and use the finder ability if you have it. Otherwise, work the bunker manually and deliberately — corners, spaces beneath larger objects, lower floor edges, and shelves you walked past dozens of times without looking closely.
