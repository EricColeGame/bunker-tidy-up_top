## What Completion Actually Means in Bunker Tidy Up

Sorting a collapsed shelter sounds relaxing until you actually see the floor. Bunker Tidy Up drops you into a bunker buried under thousands of misplaced supplies, and the only way out is one item at a time. That's why bunker tidy up completion matters more than any single session: the real progression here isn't a boss fight or a countdown timer, it's the long arc from chaos to fully labeled shelves. Understanding that arc is what keeps a cozy project from turning into a grind you quietly abandon at item 4,000.

The scale is the headline feature. Promotional material for the game advertises 17,437 supplies scattered across dozens of categories and more than 100 individual item types, and community reports from early players consistently echo that figure. It's a single-player PC release credited to Tovarishch Games, sold through [the official Bunker Tidy Up store page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=Bunker+Tidy+Up), where wishlisting and reviews give you a sense of how other players pace themselves.

Because the pile is that large, completion isn't one progress bar. It's several parallel tracks that move at different speeds — which is exactly why some players feel "almost done" at 12,000 items while others feel hopelessly stuck at 400.

| Completion Track | What You're Actually Finishing | Typical Stall Point | Clear Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor triage | Every loose item off the ground | 300–800 items | Open walking lanes |
| Shelving | Each item type on its labeled shelf | Mid-game backlog | No orphan piles |
| Category unlocks | Skills and upgrades that handle stacks | Around halfway | Stack-grab abilities working |
| Collectibles | Hidden items such as rubber ducks | Late sweep | Collectible count complete |
| Room order | The bunker looks genuinely tidy | Endgame | A clean before/after moment |

Players who treat all five tracks as one giant job burn out fast. Players who rotate between them — clearing floor, then shelving, then upgrading — tend to finish.

## The Bunker Tidy Up Progression Roadmap

Every long organizing game has a rhythm, and this one is no exception. The people who complete it fastest aren't the fastest clickers; they're the ones who change strategy at the right moment. Community reports from early access streams and clips describe three distinct phases, plus a cleanup pass that catches whatever the first three missed.

### Phase 1 — Triage (Open the Floor)

Your only goal in the opening hours is breathing room. Don't shelve yet. Pick a zone — a corner, a hallway, the area around a workbench — and drag everything into rough category clusters. Efficiency beats neatness here, because you can't plan shelf routes while you're tripping over crates.

### Phase 2 — Rhythm (The Long Middle)

This is where most of the 17,437 items disappear. Work category by category instead of room by room: grab every book, shelve every book, then move to food, then medicine, then tools. Category-first routing means fewer trips and less backtracking, and it makes the pile shrink in visible chunks rather than a slow, uniform crawl.

### Phase 3 — Refinement and the Endgame Sweep

Once the big piles are gone, the game changes character. Now you're hunting stragglers — the single battery behind a shelf, the last first aid kit in a corner you already "cleared" twice. Slow down, sweep room by room at eye level, then again at floor level, and check behind furniture. The last few percent always takes longer than the first fifty, in every game like this.

| Phase | Rough Item Range | Main Goal | Playstyle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triage | 0–500 | Open floor space | Fast, messy, zone-based |
| Rhythm | 500–12,000 | Empty the big piles | Category by category, tool-assisted |
| Refinement | 12,000–17,000 | Fill shelf gaps | Detail work, slower pace |
| Endgame sweep | Final few hundred | Find stragglers and collectibles | Systematic room-by-room search |

Because the game doesn't hand you a percentage for every sub-goal, it helps to set your own checkpoints. Use the table below as a rough framework rather than a rulebook — pace varies wildly depending on how much you enjoy the sorting itself.

## Item Categories, Shelving Logic, and Search Order

The shelf system is your map. Every shelf wants a specific kind of item, and every item has exactly one correct home, so learning the map early pays off for the rest of the run. The categories that dominate the pile are familiar ones — food, medicine, tools, books, and small tech — and each has its own shelving quirks.

| Category | Where It Usually Belongs | Sorting Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Food and canned goods | Kitchen and pantry shelves | Pull in stacks; count as you fill |
| Medicine and first aid kits | Medical shelving | Track these — total counts are limited |
| Tools and hardware | Workshop racks | Watch for near-duplicate items |
| Books and documents | Library shelves | Stacking is the community's favorite activity |
| Batteries and small tech | Utility bins | Very high count, easy to lose in piles |
| Novelty items (rubber ducks) | Hidden, not shelved normally | Treat as collectibles, not clutter |

Here's the tip that changes everything: use the Match Finder constantly. Aim at a single bag of cat food, for example, and the tool outlines every matching item in the pile while the destination shelf lights up before you arrive. That converts "searching" into "traveling," and travel is far less mentally tiring than scanning a wall of near-identical clutter. Player experience suggests this one habit can cut a long session's frustration in half.

Pair it with a rule: never walk empty-handed. If you're crossing the bunker anyway, carry something that belongs on the way.

## Tools, Skills, and Upgrades That Accelerate Completion

Progression in Bunker Tidy Up isn't only about the item count — it's also about what your character can do. Upgrades and skills reduce the number of actions needed per item, which compounds across thousands of pickups.

| Tool or Skill | What It Does | Best Used When |
|---|---|---|
| Match Finder | Outlines matching items and highlights their shelf | Any pile with 20+ identical items |
| Stack-grab skills | Pull whole stacks straight into your hands | Mid-game, once singles feel slow |
| Carry and shelving upgrades | Expand what you can move per trip | Right before a big category push |
| Shelf labeling and outlines | Shows exactly where an item lives | While learning new categories |

When you have a choice, always take the unlock that removes walking or removes clicks. Sorting speed is rarely the bottleneck — travel time is. Community walkthroughs of the first upgrade tend to agree: mobility and stack handling beat cosmetic progress every time.

## Common Completion Blockers and How to Clear Them

The last sliver of a bunker takes the longest, and almost everyone hits the same walls near the end. Knowing them in advance turns a rage-quit moment into a twenty-minute fix.

| Blocker | Why It Happens | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The missing-item problem | One unit is behind or under something | Sweep at eye level, then crouch level, then behind furniture |
| Duplicate confusion | Two items look nearly identical | Confirm with Match Finder before shelving |
| Eye fatigue | Long sessions with similar colors and shapes | Cap sessions at 45–60 minutes |
| Backtracking | Sorting by room instead of by category | Switch to category-first routing |
| Perfectionism | Re-tidying shelves you already finished | Only revisit shelves at milestones |

The single-item hunt is the most notorious. Community reports describe players sitting at 39 of 40 first aid kits, or holding one battery with hundreds still to place — and that last unit is almost never in the obvious pile. It's tucked behind a doorframe, under a staircase, or on a shelf you assumed was complete. When you're stuck, stop searching the same room harder and start searching a different room entirely.

## FAQ

**How long does bunker tidy up completion actually take?**
It depends entirely on pace. With category-first routing and heavy Match Finder use, a focused run is a multi-session project measured in tens of hours. Casual players who sort a few hundred items per evening stretch it much longer — and that's fine, since the game is designed as a relaxing wind-down activity rather than a race.

**Do I have to find every rubber duck to finish?**
Community reports treat the ducks as hidden collectibles buried under piles rather than required shelving items. If you're a completionist, slow down during the endgame sweep and check the floor beneath anything you lift. If you only care about a tidy bunker, ducks are optional.

**What should I do when I can't find the last item?**
Stop hunting and start auditing. Pick one room, clear it visually in horizontal bands from top to bottom, then check behind furniture and inside containers. If that fails, move on — the item usually turns up while you're sorting something else, because fresh piles expose surfaces you'd already written off.

**Is bunker tidy up completion easier on a second run?**
Yes, dramatically. Once you know which shelf each category belongs on, you skip the entire learning phase and route your trips efficiently from the first minute. Most players report that their second attempt feels half as long, even though the item count never changes.
