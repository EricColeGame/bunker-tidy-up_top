## How Bunker Tidy Up's Core Loop Works

Every session in Bunker Tidy Up follows the same rhythm: pick up, identify, sort, store, repeat. The individual action is simple — the difficulty is volume. With a reported 17,437 supplies to organize across a complete playthrough, an improvised approach buries you under your own good intentions.

Community reports agree on one thing: define a route before you touch a single crate. Players who sort first and carry second tend to clear rooms far faster than those who haul items around while deciding where each one belongs.

- **Recon (2–3 minutes):** Walk the room, note the categories present, and count the containers.
- **Extraction:** Empty one container at a time and stage its contents nearby — never mix two containers mid-sort.
- **Grouping:** Build category piles on the floor or in a staging zone.
- **Placement:** Move finished piles to their permanent shelves in one trip.
- **Reset:** Sweep the floor for stragglers before moving to the next room.

| Stage | What You're Doing | Why It Saves Time |
|---|---|---|
| Recon | Surveying the room before touching anything | Prevents mid-run backtracking |
| Extraction | Emptying containers one by one | Keeps categories from blending |
| Grouping | Staging piles by type | Turns many small decisions into one |
| Placement | Bulk-moving sorted piles | Fewer trips, fewer mistakes |
| Reset | Final sweep for missed items | Stops "almost done" rooms from lingering |

The reason this loop works is that it separates **deciding** from **carrying**. Most clutter games punish players who do both at once, because every new item on the floor is a fresh decision competing for your attention.

## Bunker Tidy Up Walkthrough: Early Game Steps

The first hour sets the tone for your entire playthrough. New players typically try to clean everything at once and end up with six half-finished rooms and no storage space. Follow this order instead.

| Step | Action | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear the entrance and main walkway | Restores movement space |
| 2 | Claim one room as a staging area | Gives you a place to think |
| 3 | Sort the smallest container cluster first | Early wins build momentum |
| 4 | Learn your category labels before stacking | Wrong labels cost double work |
| 5 | Fill shelves back-to-front, heavy items low | Maximizes usable space |
| 6 | Bank progress by finishing one full room | Creates a visual anchor |

Starting with the smallest cluster is the single most underrated tip in any bunker tidy up walkthrough. A finished small room teaches you the category system while the stakes are low, and it gives you a clean reference point for how a completed zone should look.

## Room-by-Room Priorities and Sorting Routes

Not all clutter deserves the same urgency. Prioritize rooms that block movement or storage capacity, and leave cosmetic tidying for last.

| Room Type | Typical Clutter | Priority | Best Practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry / corridor | Loose crates, floor debris | High | Clear floor space first, always |
| Main storage hall | Mixed categories, deep shelves | High | One category per shelf run |
| Food and supplies | Small, numerous items | Medium | Group by shelf life, not size |
| Equipment bay | Bulky gear, awkward shapes | Medium | Bottom shelves for heavy items |
| Workshop | Tools, scattered parts | Low | Sort by task, not by tool type |
| Personal quarters | Miscellaneous junk | Low | Finish only after storage is stable |

### Build a Route, Not a Routine

A route is a fixed path you repeat every time you enter a room; a routine is whatever you feel like doing. Routes win.

- Always enter a room and sweep the same direction — left to right, or clockwise.
- Keep your staging zone in the same spot so your hands learn the distance.
- Never start a second room while the first still has items on the floor.
- Batch similar actions: all labeling, then all lifting, then all stacking.

This is where most bunker tidy up walkthrough content gets vague, so treat the table above as your default order and adjust it only when a specific room forces you to.

## Skills, Categories, and Storage Upgrades

As you progress, Bunker Tidy Up offers skills and upgrades that change how fast you can work. Exact names vary, but player experience points to four broad buckets worth investing in.

| Upgrade Type | What It Improves | When to Invest |
|---|---|---|
| Handling speed | Pick up and place actions | Early — compounds all run long |
| Carrying capacity | Items moved per trip | Mid-game, once rooms get dense |
| Identification | Faster category recognition | Whenever clutter grows varied |
| Stamina / energy | Sustains long cleanup sessions | Before tackling the largest rooms |

A useful rule from community reports: never spend on capacity before handling speed. Moving two items quickly beats moving four items slowly when your bottleneck is decision-making, not distance.

Categories matter just as much. The game's supply list is enormous, but nearly everything collapses into a handful of families. If a category pile grows past a comfortable size, split it by **function** rather than by name — "lighting," "fuel," "medical," and so on. Players who split by function report fewer mislabeled shelves and much faster retrieval later.

## Common Mistakes, Speed Tips, and Reaching the Ending

Even experienced organizers fall into the same traps on a fresh playthrough. Here are the ones that cost the most time.

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Do This Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Sorting everything at once | Nothing ever looks finished | Complete one room fully |
| Mixing categories in staging | Creates a second mess | One pile per category |
| Ignoring labels until the end | Forces a re-sort of everything | Label as you stack |
| Chasing the biggest room first | Fatigue sets in before progress | Warm up on small zones |
| Refusing to backtrack at all | Leaves orphan items everywhere | Budget one final sweep per room |

The ending deserves a note of its own. Bunker Tidy Up rewards a **finished** bunker, not a mostly-finished one. Community reports suggest that partial cleanup stalls progress, so the last stretch is about closing out loose ends: orphan items, unsorted containers, and any room you skipped because it looked intimidating.

For players who prefer a quieter experience, a no-commentary approach works well — the game's visual language is clear enough that you can follow a route without narration. ## FAQ

**How long does a full bunker tidy up walkthrough take?**
It depends heavily on your route discipline. Players who recon rooms before sorting, batch their trips, and finish one zone at a time report dramatically shorter runs than those who clean reactively. Expect the later rooms to take longer simply because there is more to categorize.

**Do I need to organize all 17,437 supplies to see the ending?**
The reported supply count represents everything across a complete playthrough. The ending triggers on a finished, organized bunker state rather than on a specific item tally, but skipping rooms usually delays that state rather than shortening it.

**What should I do when I get stuck in a cluttered room?**
Stop carrying and start sorting. Clear a staging area, empty one container, group by category, and place the finished pile before touching the next container. Getting unstuck is almost always a routing problem, not a puzzle problem.

**Is a no-commentary walkthrough better for learning the game?**
For most players, yes — a bunker tidy up walkthrough without commentary keeps your attention on the room layout and sorting order, which is exactly what you need to copy. Use commentary versions when you want to understand *why* a route was chosen.
