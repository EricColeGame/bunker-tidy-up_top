## What You Actually Do in a Bunker Tidy Up Session

The loop is simple to describe and surprisingly hard to put down. You spawn into a post-apocalyptic bunker, pick up supplies one at a time, and place them where the game's internal logic says they belong. There are no timers, no score penalties, and no fail state — just a very large pile and a lot of shelves.

The work is organized around supply categories. Food, medicine, tools, and books are the big buckets, and each individual item has its own placement rules. That's what separates Bunker Tidy Up from a mindless sorting chore: a tin of something edible and a box of bandages might both be "small and metal," but they belong in completely different rooms.

| Category | Typical Contents | What Players Get Wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food | Cans, jars, dry goods, ration packs | Sorting by package size instead of shelf type |
| Medicine | Bandages, bottles, first-aid supplies | Mixing with household chemicals |
| Tools | Hand tools, hardware, repair kits | Storing heavy items high up |
| Books | Paperbacks, manuals, reference volumes | Treating weight as irrelevant |

Because there are more than 100 unique item types, brute force stops working fast. You need a system.

## Practical Tips for Your First Few Hours

Community reports and the game's own design both point to the same conclusion — start with structure, not with speed. Here's a priority order that works.

| Priority | Action | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Clear one empty stretch of shelf as a landing zone | Gives you somewhere to drop items while you decide |
| 2 | Pick one category and finish it completely | Prevents half-sorted piles in every room |
| 3 | Group by function, not by appearance | Matches the game's placement logic and avoids rework |
| 4 | Sweep the floor and corners regularly | Catches hidden-object items early |
| 5 | Save when you finish a room | You can save anytime, so use it |
| 6 | Revisit skills after patches | Buffed skills change what's efficient |

### Build a Mental Map Before You Sort

Walk the bunker once without picking anything up. Note which shelves sit near the entrance, which areas are dark, and which rooms are already partly stocked. Ten minutes of scouting saves hours of hauling, because you'll stop carrying food three rooms in the wrong direction.

### Let the Lack of Timers Work for You

The "play at your own pace" design is not just marketing. With no quick-time events and no clock, you can leave a half-organized room and come back to it days later. Players who treat the game as a series of short, calm sessions tend to report a better experience than those who try to clear everything in one sitting.

### Chase Achievements as a Sorting Guide

The 14 Steam achievements function as a soft roadmap. Names like Archivist, Storekeeper, Nothing Forgotten, and Jack of All Trades hint at what the developers consider complete play — full category coverage, thorough exploration, and no items left behind. If you're the type who likes goals, use them as your checklist.
