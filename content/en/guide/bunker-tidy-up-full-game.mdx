## How the Bunker Tidy Up Full Game Sorting Loop Actually Works

Every session follows the same rhythm: identify, categorize, carry, place, repeat. What makes it more interesting than a mindless pick-up quest is that the Bunker Tidy Up full game gives more than 100 unique items their own placement rules. You are not just filling shelves — you are building a logic system that has to hold up across dozens of supply categories.

The broad categories are easy to remember because they mirror a real emergency shelter: food, medicine, tools, and books. Each one has a natural home, and keeping those homes consistent is what separates a tidy bunker from a room full of random crates.

| Category | Typical Home | Why Consistency Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food and rations | Pantry shelving | Keeps perishables and cans clustered for quick counting |
| Medicine | Medical cabinet or clinic shelf | Small items disappear fast in open storage |
| Tools | Workshop racks | Heavy items are easier to shelve near where they are used |
| Books and records | Archive or library shelves | Supports cataloging-style achievements |
| Mixed supplies | Staging zone | Prevents you from dumping unknowns into sorted aisles |

### A First-Hour Routine That Prevents a Mess Later

New players often try to sort everything at once and burn out. A better approach is to work in contained zones so that every session ends with visible progress.

| Step | Action | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pick one room or shelf bank | Creates a bounded task instead of an endless one |
| 2 | Clear the floor of that zone first | Reveals hidden items and walkable paths |
| 3 | Sort by category, then by size | Makes placement decisions faster |
| 4 | Move unknowns to a staging pile | Avoids stalling on tricky items |
| 5 | Revisit the staging pile at the end | Turns leftovers into a short, focused task |

Because the game lets you save at any time, you can stop mid-zone without losing progress. Use that: a 30-minute tidy-up session is far more sustainable than a four-hour marathon across the whole shelter.

## Actionable Tips for a Faster, Tidier Bunker

Speed in the Bunker Tidy Up full game comes from better decisions, not faster clicking. These habits consistently pay off.

| Tip | Why It Works | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finish one shelf before starting another | Half-sorted aisles are the main source of rework | Low |
| Sort frequent-use items at eye level | Reduces travel time on repeat visits | Low |
| Keep a single staging zone | Stops unknowns from polluting clean zones | Low |
| Empty the floor before shelving | Reveals items hidden behind props | Medium |
| Batch similar items by hand | Fewer trips, fewer placement mistakes | Medium |
| Set a session goal | Prevents open-ended wandering | Low |

A few additional pointers from general organizing-game habits:

- Use the camera comfortably. Camera comfort options exist in the settings, so tweak sensitivity before a long session.
- Sort in the order you naturally move. If you always enter through the same door, start there and work inward.
- Play at your own pace. The game has no quick-time events and no pressure systems, so there is no reason to rush placement decisions.
- Take breaks. With 17,437 items, fatigue is the real enemy — not difficulty.

The most common mistake players make is treating the bunker as one giant task. It is not. It is a few hundred small tasks, and each finished shelf is a completed win.
